Fusion Dashboard Settings

Opening the Dashboard reading...
Opening this page can mean two different things, and this switch decides which. With it ON, the Dashboard treats your visit as an intent to work: it launches Fusion on the selected box if it is closed, drives the Autodesk sign-in as far as code can (stopping at your password and telling you), turns on Autodesk's MCP server, and signs in to APS cloud search when a client id is already configured. Every step runs in the BACKGROUND and narrates itself as a toast; nothing steals your focus. With it OFF, the Dashboard only reports status and acts on nothing until you press a button.
Get everything ready when I open the Dashboard
Default: On
Fusion upkeep
Two chores the bridge does for you on the machine itself. Auto-update clicks Fusion's own "Update Now" nag in the background whenever Autodesk shows it, so you always run the latest build and never see the prompt. Seat take-over answers Autodesk's "Active Sessions Exceeded" dialog when your one seat is busy on another machine: it clicks through both dialogs in the background, taking the seat here while the other machine's Fusion suspends RESUMABLY, and toasts you once so you know. Both are checked by a background sweep every 20 seconds, and both choices are sticky per user.
Silently install Fusion updates
Default: On
Take over the Autodesk seat when it is busy elsewhere
Default: On
Taskbar identity
While Adom is driving Fusion, its Windows taskbar button wears a small Adom mark in the corner, so a glance at the taskbar tells you why Fusion is busy. The badge is painted cross-process through Adom Bridge's desktop_taskbar verb, appears on the first driving verb, and sheds itself after five minutes with no driving activity (status polls do not count, or it would never clear). Turning this off removes the badge immediately and permanently.
Show the "Adom is driving" badge on Fusion's taskbar button
Default: On
Activity indicator
How Fusion's taskbar button signals that Adom is running a verb. Progress bar paints a real bar paced by how long this machine has actually taken for that verb, and clears the moment it finishes or fails - so you can tell "nearly done" from "just started" at a glance. Flash is the older attention flash: it says something is happening, and nothing more. A verb with no measured history yet runs the bar indeterminate rather than inventing a percentage. Run times are recorded per verb to verb-times.jsonl; fusion_verb_times reports p50/p90 per verb.
Default: Progress bar
